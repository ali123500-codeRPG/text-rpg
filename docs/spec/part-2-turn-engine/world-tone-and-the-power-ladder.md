### World, tone and the power ladder

Settled by Ali 14/11/2026 ("Tone, land and the power ladder", TL1–TL29). All of it is a placeholder until a much later stage (TL27), and stands only until magic and mana are built, which will become core to the game (TL23, D-332). The agents' short world lore and the Narrator's tone line carry the short form; this section holds the full pictures for design and balance. Every question on the page is answered.

- **Voice (TL1 a).** Close on the hero's shoulder, after the novel Surviving the Game as a Barbarian: short lines, one thought each; the telling sizes everything up as threat, price or use; dry, never solemn.
- **Big moments (TL2 d).** Short lines that count the odds, one brutal act, one flat line after. No inner speeches, no world holding its breath.
- **A fight the hero far outclasses (TL28 a).** Casual and fast: over in a few lines, told as a chore, and the telling shrinks as the gap widens. It never savours the blows.
- **The supernatural (TL5 a).** Told only through results, in the same shoulder voice: what the eye cannot follow, a sound that comes late, damage too great, a fear felt in the body. No light, no visible aura, no named techniques. On the casual dark sample here Ali cut the closing remark about the aftermath: the lightness of a dark scene sits in the act and the people in it, and no wry line is added after. **Changed by IL4 b** (Ali, 16/11/2026, "World lore as an isekai fantasy world"): the supernatural may be visible where the game data names it (aura skills today, spells once D-332 lands); nothing else glows, and no technique is invented.
- **People (TL6 a).** Flexible morals. People are warm and practical: they help, lie, steal or sell someone out as their needs call for, without malice and without guilt. Someone can help the hero in the morning and sell them at noon, and mean both. A person's needs and stance decide what they do, never a label of good or evil.
- **Dark scenes (TL3 d).** Told casually, after FFF-Class Trashero: death and cruelty are on the page, fast, as plain business, often with a dry joke on top. Never lingering, never solemn. Nobody under the Youth start exists (D-316).
- **Humour (TL4 d).** Characters joke dryly, and the telling carries the hero's own running commentary beside the danger, after Kumoko. The commentary follows the hero's resolved state (nerve, morale, wounds, personality line) and never decides an action. Built with D-334 (PR #867): Narrator rule 16 asks for it, shaped by the view's player.personality (Nerve line first), morale, health and wounds; see Agent Narrator.
- **The age (TL8 a).** Fantasy, about 1400 in our terms: plate, crossbows, walled towns, mills, guilds and coin; no gunpowder, no printing. Another country is advanced in magic and lives closer to 1950; it waits with D-332, and the agents are not told of it until then. **The look only, not lore** (Ali NR37 and IL1 a, 16/11/2026): the world is an anime-style isekai fantasy world with game-like rules (levels, skills, monsters that grow stronger) that only looks about 1400. The agents' lore describes the genre in its own words and never names Kumoko, whose System and higher beings would clash with the small gods and the secret Thread. Whether people speak of levels and skills (IL2) is still being discussed.
- **Who holds the land (TL7 c).** Under the Great. Ordinary lords and reeves run daily life, but borders lie where the reach of someone past the mortal limit ends, and every place knows who stands over it. So a rising hero always has someone above them.
- **How wide the land is (TL13 c).** No picture of the whole yet: regions are drawn as needed. A placeholder.
- **Peoples (TL9 a, TL10 a, TL11 a).** Elves live among humans as a small minority, with a few old holds of their own; they live for centuries and remember. Dragons are rare great beasts: clever, never speaking, each known by the place it holds. People in dragon shape are a separate humanoid race, not built (D-330). Slimes are mindless vermin of wet ground: common, slow, deadly in numbers.
- **The wild (TL12 a).** Weather, distance, real beasts and outlaws. Monsters are few kinds, each with its place, and the agents invent no new kinds. To revisit once the higher levels are built (D-331). **Changed by IL6 b** (Ali, 16/11/2026, "World lore as an isekai fantasy world": "b, we can look into the details later"): monsters roam beyond the walls and grow stronger the deeper the wild goes; dungeons and lairs exist. Kinds still come only from the game data. The details (which kinds, how deep, what a lair holds) wait on D-331.
- **Magic (TL14).** Parked (D-332). Until then the agents are told to invent no spells and no spellcasters, as a guard and not as a ruling on the world.
- **Gods in daily life (TL15 a).** Worship is small and practical: wayside shrines, a coin before a risk. No church and no priests with power. Only the gods in the library are named; otherwise "the gods".
- **Echoes (TL16 a).** Now and then someone walks the roads who never changes, never learns and cannot be parted from their gear; some wear the face of the famous dead. People call them echoes and do not know why they come. Nobody in the world has heard of other worlds; the Universe and world layers exist only in the saves.
- **The climb past 100 (TL17 a).** Every 50 points past 100 doubles what a body can do: 150 is twice the mortal limit, 200 four times, 300 sixteen, 400 sixty-four, with no top. The starting point at 100, a little past today's records: a lift of about 600 kg (record 510 kg), a sprint of 15 m/s (record peak 12.4 m/s; muscle limit estimated at 15.6 m/s or more, Weyand 2010), a 3 m jump (record 2.45 m). Force and jump height double per 50; sprint speed grows by the square root of two. Nothing in play doubles: base sight, hearing, damage and health do not grow with skill past 100 (CR5 to CR9) and the dice past 100 get no ceiling rule (D-213 closed: more bands past Critical open only by ways the owner grants), so the curve is the target the perk rows at 120, 140 and up should add up to, not a formula.
- **The ladder (TL18 to TL23 a, TL29 a).** Each picture is the whole person at that level: skill, the perks its milestones bring, and gear.
  - **50, Trained.** A working professional: a gate guard six winters in, a journeyman smith. Runs 100 m in about 14 s, carries a 30 kg pack all day. Even odds against another like them; beats an untrained man most times. Nobody looks twice.
  - **75, Elite.** The real record book: 100 m in 9.58 s, a 510 kg lift, a 2.45 m high jump. A guard loses to them nearly every time; two guards at once is an even fight. Lords pay to keep them close.
  - **100, Paragon.** The limit of a human body: past every record, yet nothing a body could not do. Three or four guards at once, and wins; ten with spears and room still bring one down. The last step training alone can reach.
  - **150, Beyond.** Twice the limit. Lifts 1.2 tonnes, clears a 6 m palisade, runs down a galloping horse. A patrol of ten is not a fight and a barred door is not a door; twenty crossbows with cover and a plan are a real danger. Three or four Paragons together are an even match. Met with awe, fear or greed, never as a freak.
  - **200, Mythic.** Four times the limit. Lifts 2.4 tonnes, clears a castle's curtain wall (about 10 m), crosses a 10 km region in under six minutes. A company of a hundred breaks rather than dies; walls and gates do not count. Three or four Beyond together are an even match. Realms plan around them like weather.
  - **300, Transcendent (TL29 a, a placeholder).** Sixteen times the limit, a new tier between Mythic and Divine: someone who has left the mortal world's scale without becoming a god. Lifts about 10 tonnes, clears a 50 m cliff, crosses a region in under three minutes. An army is not a fight and a fortress is not a wall. Three or four Mythic together are an even match. Realms move their borders round one. A handful in the whole world. Built as a placeholder rank, rank\_transcendent, anchor 300, level 50 (D-335).
  - **400, Divine.** Sixty-four times the limit: the lowest rung of the divine, a god and not a person. Mortal arms do not count at all. Inside its narrow field a god's word is final; outside it, a god is only very strong. Almost never seen.
- **Getting past 100 (TL25 a).** No one trains past the mortal limit. The Great are people who lived through deeds that should have killed them and took strong essences from strong foes; every one has a story.
- **How many stand at each level (TL24 a).** A step per place, as minimums and not hard limits: a village has at least a Veteran or an Elite, a town at least a Paragon, a city at least one of the Beyond, a realm's court at least a Mythic, and the world a handful of the Transcendent. More may stand in any of them. The gods are not found by walking. What counts is who lives in the places a hero visits, not a census. From Beyond up, NPC presets skip the skill cap (D-314 c, a placeholder), so a city's Beyond plays at 150 and a court's Mythic at 200.
- **Words (TL26 a).** People use no ranks. They say what they saw, and call anyone past the mortal limit one of the Great (a stand-in word). The rank names stay game-side; an order or a watch may still rank its own through its theme's titles. **Changed by IL2 a and IL5 b** (Ali, 16/11/2026): everyone knows their own level and skills and speaks of them openly; another person's level is only what they claim, what their deeds show, or a guess (an appraisal skill may come later). The Narrator may name levels and skills; no agent is given level data for speech yet. "The Great" stays the word for anyone past 100, and the very strongest carry famous titles the whole land knows (IL5 b, details parked as D-348). **Rank words stay game-side** (MU8 c, Ali 16/11/2026, "Mushoku Tensei ideas for our game"): nobody in the world says Novice, Elite, Paragon and so on, even though the two ladders match (Mushoku Tensei's Beginner to God sits on Novice to Transcendent, and its Saint, where battle aura begins, on our 100). A title is a fixed name on a unique NPC and cannot be taken (MU10 b); there is no ranked list of the strongest (MU12 a).
- **Use (TL27 a).** One line per level goes into the agents' world lore; the pictures above are the yardstick for perk rows and NPC ranks. A report-only check of the rank presets against them (a guard against an Elite, three guards against a Paragon) is its own piece of work.
