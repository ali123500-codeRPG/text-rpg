### Scenarios

A scenario is the stretch of play between two scenario starts.

- **A scenario is a situation** (Scenes Deep Dive SC1–SC10, Ali, 11/11/2026): a stretch of play around one hidden question, such as "will the player get the debt back from the smugglers?". The Weaver's scenario plan gives the question, its stake (D-339), a scale and two or three endings (`scenario`, SCENARIO\_ENDINGS\_MIN 2 to SCENARIO\_ENDINGS\_MAX 3: always more than one way out). A plan without a scenario, with an unknown scale or with the wrong number of endings is a routine error.
  - **Scales** (names table SCENARIO\_SCALES, so a scale can be inserted between two others; levers in CONFIG.SCENARIO\_SCALE, all placeholders): Moment (12 turns, 1 place), Situation (40 turns, 2 places), Episode (120 turns, 4 places), Venture (3 days, any number of places). The Weaver picks the scale at any tier (SC3); code raises a scale too small for the places the plan builds and notes it (scenario\_scale\_raised). The turn budgets are not used yet (SC12, step 2).
  - **Endings** (SC7) reuse the quest goal checks (one shared function each): band\_out (every member of a band down, broken or gone from the stage), reach a place, leave a place (once the player has been in it), find someone, hold an item, deliver an item to someone, a time of day, clear a place (BF94 a: nobody hostile to the player still standing in a place or zone, from any band, counted out as band\_out counts a band's members; met only after a foe has stood there in this scenario, ScenarioFrame.hadFoes), or judged (only the Weaver can tell; code never meets it). Each ending says how the scenario ends, from the names table SCENARIO\_ENDINGS: resolved, lost, left, escaped, interrupted, worn out, broken off (the last two code-only, SC10), with a line of what happened. An ending that names nothing real is dropped after a failed repair. Every scenario with a judged ending files a bug, scenario\_ending\_judged, so the endings code could not check are reviewed (Ali: a bug whenever code can't judge).
  - **When a scenario ends.** Code checks the scenario's endings after every turn, and the bigger scenario's too (below). The first one met is kept (scenario\_ending\_met), but it is held while the player is fighting, being chased (a pursuer, CH4) or in a talk that turn (SC13, scenario\_ending\_held); the scenario ends on the first clear turn (breakCause scenario\_end). It also ends at a story beat with endsScenario or a stall (worn out: STALL\_TURNS, *placeholder*, 8 quiet turns in a row with no beat left whose owner is active), when the player is knocked out (broken off), and a road encounter sets it aside (RE7, Inner scenarios below). **Rest, sleep, waiting and time skips never end a scenario** (Ali, SC4/SC9).
  - **Inner scenarios** (SC4). An Episode or Venture (CONFIG.SCENARIO\_HOLDS\_FROM) holds smaller scenarios one level deep: a road encounter inside one starts an inner scenario and keeps the bigger one as `outer`. When the inner scenario ends, the bigger one carries on with no new event roll and no Weaver call (scenario\_resumed). An ending of the bigger scenario met during the inner one ends both; being knocked out ends both. **A road encounter never ends the running scenario (RE7, Ali 17/11/2026):** with no bigger scenario, the running one (whatever its scale) becomes `outer`; already inside one, it waits as `paused` (its growth held like the bigger one's) and carries on when the encounter ends; a second encounter meanwhile replaces the first, and the paused one still waits. A knock-out or the bigger scenario's ending ends all of them.
  - **The Episode and its parts** (D-342, SW5 a, SW10 a on the "Scenario event rolls" page, Ali, 15/11/2026). With no bigger scenario running, a scenario start plans two things in one Weaver call: `episode`, an Episode, Journey or Venture with its own question, stake and endings built from the open threads, and `scenario`, its first part (a moment or situation). Code keeps the episode as `outer` (episode\_planned; each has its own scenario number) and checks: no episode with a moment or situation start is a repair error (episode\_missing), an episode smaller than an Episode is episode\_scale, and a first part of Episode size or more is scenario\_scale\_inner. A plan whose scenario is itself an Episode or bigger needs no episode. When a part ends, play is back in the Episode **between parts** (stage.between, after the rest of WB7): the same things that start a scenario in free roam start the next part inside it (danger at once; a job, a goal reached, a stale job or a quiet stretch as a window the Weaver may decline), each with its own event roll. Nothing closes when a part is called for: the Episode becomes `outer` when the part begins (episode\_inner), its growth held until its own end. Between parts the stall does not run. The Episode holds the ground of every part, ends by its own endings or budget, and then free roam starts.
  - **Scenario records** (SC6). When a scenario ends, code keeps a record in hidden state (scenarios, the last SCENARIO\_RECORDS\_SAVED 30): its number, question, scale, tier, ending and line, its turns, who fell (anyone up at the start who is down now; the player when knocked out), and the items the player gained. The next Weaver sees the last SCENARIO\_RECORDS\_SHOWN (5) as lastScenarios and writes the aftermath from them.
  - **Weaver check-in** (SC8, SC14). A background Weaver call (its own instructions, WEAVER\_CHECKIN) about the running scenario, started after a turn is saved, so nobody waits for it. It is asked at set moments: the scenario reaches its budget (once), a beat fires, or the scenario has judged endings (every CHECKIN\_GAP\_TURNS, 5); at most CHECKIN\_MAX\_PER\_SCENARIO (6) per scenario and 5 turns apart (placeholders); the over-time check-in (budget reached) always happens, never stopped by that cap and not counted toward it (Ali, 12/11/2026, BG-42). It sees the scenario's question, endings, turns and budget used, what has happened in it (ScenarioFrame.happened: code writes one hidden line per spar result, yield, defeat, give, trade, job and deal, the newest CONFIG.SCENARIO\_HAPPENED\_MAX (30, placeholder) kept; BG-41) and the memory lines people on the stage kept since it began, and answers continue (with new beats, triggers owned by actors on the stage) or end (with the ending it reached and a hidden line). The answer applies at the next turn it is back for, to the scenario it was asked about (dropped if that scenario is over); an end waits for a clear turn like any ending (SC13). A failed or invalid answer changes nothing, files a bug (checkin\_failed) and the next moment asks again. **SC12 (Ali, 11/11/2026):** a continue may also change the scenario: a new question, scale or endings (`change`; endings replace the list, checked like a plan's, and the scenario keeps its number, start and records); send people in through ways that already exist (`arrivals`: bands, cast and seeds as in a stage plan, each starting on a stage zone the player cannot see now and walking in from there, never placed by their routine); and add frontiers on stage zones (`frontiers`: {zone, key, label}, a hidden door or a cart track, built later by the normal stage extension). It never adds zones or rooms to a place already seen: the world grows only through frontiers. One bad part drops the whole answer (checkin\_failed). Built in src/scenario/checkin.ts on applyStagePlan's checkin kind.
  - **Code endings** (SC12). The budget leaves out turns spent fighting. At SCENARIO\_OVERRUN\_MULT (2) times its budget, code ends the scenario as worn out, after any fight or chase; the stall floor stays for every scale. Both file a bug (scenario\_overrun, scenario\_stalled), since the check-in should have ended the scenario first.
  - **Stuck** (SC15). /stuck [what is wrong] files a bug (player\_stuck) and asks a check-in at once, past its gap and limit, telling the Weaver the player is stuck: it must give a way forward (a beat or an ending) and never removes a danger. The GM view has an End scenario button (asks to confirm): the scenario ends after the next turn, recorded as interrupted.
  - **The ending moment** (SC11, SD1). As soon as a turn's resolve ends a scenario, before the next scenario's Weaver and the Narrator run, the game says how it ended (GameApi scenarioEnding(): the SCENARIO\_ENDINGS key and its name), so the play page can start the ending moment while those calls run (no false delay). The Narrator is told the ending's name only (NarrationView scenarioEnded, never the hidden question or ending line) and gives one closing sentence for the scenario that ended in `closing` (rule 20), apart from `text`, which tells the rest of the turn and the next opening; a closing on any other turn is dropped, and it is leak-checked with the text. The turn's narration line carries scenarioEnd {as, name, closing}, so the moment shows again after a reload. The play page builds the motion: the closing line, then the scenario break carrying the ending's name in its colour, then the opening.
  A frontier crossing in the same turn goes first; the break waits for the next turn.
- **Barriers on ways** (Scenes Round Three, Ali 11/11/2026: SD3–SD5, BA2–BA8; src/engine/barriers.ts). A way between two zones may carry a barrier: a door, gate, grate, hatch or rubble (library barrierKinds, BK1–BK5: each says whether it takes a lock or a bar, whether it is a blockage, whether sight passes it). One record per barrier (GameState.barriers) serves both sides; each side's link names it by id. Its grade (none to extreme) is how hard it is to pick and how stout it is. No barrier is impassable (SD4): a key, a pick, a word with the guard, or breaking it.
  - **Shut and passing.** A barrier is shut while it is locked, barred or blocked, unless it is broken through: Destroyed is open to everyone, and Crippled lets an amorphous body through (BA7). Walking paths for a walker go round a way shut to it (shortestPath with a walker); distances for sound and sight ignore barriers. When no other way exists, the walker walks up to it and tries it: the move is spent, the event says it was found locked, barred or blocked, and the walker knows it from then on (Barrier.tried). An NPC tries a shut way once.
  - **Open doors** (BG-70, Ali 12/11/2026). A door, gate, grate or hatch can stand open: the Parser's way op "open" (in the move slot) opens it without going through, turning the player's key or sliding the bar on their side as it goes; then sight passes the link as if it were a sight link (relations seesAcross), and views mark it open. Op "shut" shuts it again. Opening one shut against you is trying it (found locked, barred or blocked). An open barrier is never shut; it must be shut before it is locked or barred. NPCs don't open or shut ways yet.
  - **Keys** (BA3). A key is an item (IT40) whose instance names the barriers it opens (ItemInstance.opens). Walking through with it turns the lock in the move slot, and the walk goes on next turn. Locking or unlocking on purpose is the Parser's way op (lock, unlock), which takes the move. The Weaver names who carries a barrier's key (keys); keys can be bought, stolen or looted like any item.
  - **Bars** (BA4). A bar is on one side (bar.side). From that side it slides back in the move slot on the way through, or across or back on purpose (way op bar, unbar). From the other side the way is shut: break it (Breaking walls and barriers, below), or get someone inside to open it.
  - **Blocked** (BA5). Rubble is shut to all until it is cleared; clearing it is breaking it (below), with the rubble material and its depth as thickness.
- **Breaking walls and barriers** (Ali 18/11/2026, Questions hub page "Fantasy breakable walls": BW1, BW8–BW14, BW16–BW26; src/ruleset/breaking.ts). Breaking follows the strength of whoever breaks, and walls built by mortals do not exist for a Transcendent (BW1). Nothing in it rolls (BW23 a), and like every rating check it teaches no skill (GR25 c).
  - **Strength** (BW18, BW25 b). The breaker's strength is the strength rating the action uses: strengthFor 'break', the best of Melee strength × 1 and Ranged strength × 0.5 (CONFIG.STRENGTH_KINDS), read as it is.
  - **Lift** (BW19 a). Strength is a lift in kg, one curve: straight lines through 0 → 0, 50 → 250 kg (a placeholder for a working professional), 75 → 510 kg and 100 → 600 kg (the power ladder's anchors, TL17), then doubling every 50 with no top (CONFIG.LIFT_KG_AT, LIFT_DOUBLING_PER). It is the one base number that keeps doubling in play, by Ali's choice, against TL17's "nothing in play doubles".
  - **Time** (BW20 a). time = the material's time at 50 × thickness ÷ its example thickness × ((lift at 50 + BREAK_TOOL_KG) ÷ (lift + tool kg))^BREAK_FORCE_POWER, power 3 (placeholder). A professional at strength 50 with a tool takes the table's time; strength 150 takes about 1/27 of it bare-handed, 200 about 1/216, 300 about 1/13,800.
  - **Tools** (BW22 a). A sledgehammer or pick (IT87, IT88, tag break_tool) adds BREAK_TOOL_KG (150 kg) to the force; with Shield Breaker (Heavy weapons 20) a heavy melee weapon in hand counts as one. The kilos matter most in the human range and fade past 100.
  - **Materials** (BW24 a, BW8 a; library barrierMaterials, BM). Each row: real thickness range, example thickness, time at 50 (a professional with the right tool, person-sized hole), noise per blow, an optional trait, and its source. Everyday rows: wattle and daub 15 cm 1.5 min, timber planks 5 cm 1.5 min (a stud wall with wood panel, NCEL TN-1425 Table 1), oak 5 cm 5 min (EN 1627 RC3), brick 20 cm 2 min (8-in mortar-filled block, NCEL TN-1425 Table 3), iron bars 3 cm 10 min (EN 1627 RC4), stone 20 cm 11 min (nearest source: 8-in concrete, NCEL TN-1425 Table 3, NBSIR 73-101; a cottage wall of 60 cm is 33 min, a 2.5 m castle curtain wall about 2.3 h), glass 0.5 cm 1 s (estimate), rubble 1 m 17.5 min (shovels to the cubic yard, EngineersEdge; 3 s a shovel is an estimate). Fantasy rows (placeholders): ice, living wood and slime membrane (both regrow), lair stone (harder the deeper the wild goes, waits on D-331), starmetal (about four months at 50). No warded stone until magic exists (BW9 b, D-332).
  - **Default materials** (BW26 a). A wall that names no material reads its wall kind's (wall and low wall brick, glazed glass, arcade stone, fence timber planks) at that material's example thickness, since the plan's 0.25 m is only a drawing width; a wall that names one reads its own thicknessM. A barrier with no material reads its kind's: door, gate and hatch oak, grate and barred window iron, shutters timber planks, arrow slit stone, rubble rubble; a barrier's thicknessCm, when given, replaces the example thickness.
  - **Walking through** (BW21 a). When breaking would take WALK_THROUGH_S (2 s) or less, the wall or barrier does not stop the walker: a walk whose straight line to where it is going (on one floor) meets only things the walker passes or walks through goes straight on, breaking each as it reaches it within that leg of the walk (a hole a doorway's size in a wall, a barrier Destroyed), each with its breakthrough noise (event break\_through, walked). A wall is broken only when the straight line is shorter than the walk round it (a door beside it is used instead); a shut barrier on the line is always broken, the doorway being the way anyway. Every mortal-built wall is walked through by 300; starmetal and deep lair stone still stop a Transcendent. Code paths and the views still treat a barrier as shut until it is broken (src/engine/breakThrough.ts walkBreaks).
  - **Wear and holes** (BW11 a, BW12 a, BW14 a). Halfway through its time a wall is Crippled: a see-through opening at the spot (Opening.crack, heard through as an open gap) that only an amorphous body passes; done, it is Destroyed: a gap opening a doorway's size (CONFIG.BREAK\_HOLE\_M, 0.9 × 2 m, no higher than the wall) anyone walks through, joining the titles on either side, or a frontier opening where nothing is built behind (src/plan/holes.ts). Any solid wall kind can be broken (wall, glazed, arcade, low wall, fence), and every barrier on a way; a barrier takes the same two states. A hole stays, saved in the place's floor plan like any way; nothing repairs it; mending walls belongs with the living world (D-67, D-68; D-89 RP11 a).
  - **Time, helpers and interruptions** (BW13 a). Breaking is a long action, as picking: out of a fight the time passes at once and stops when someone comes into view; in a fight each turn adds a turn of work. Several breakers add their rates (1 ÷ time each). Work on a material that regrows is lost after CONFIG.REGROW\_S (a minute) with nobody at it, and its crack closes (BW9 b). Each full spell of work (BREAK\_SPELL\_S) with a tool or weapon is one breakage check on it by the BK rules: a d100 at the breaker's strength against its fragility's line, so a tough sledgehammer breaks only past Critical Failure (BW23 a).
  - **Noise** (BW10 a). Each turn of work makes the material's noise per blow (event break\_through); the turn it breaks through is one step louder. The Narrator tells how far through it is in words and never shows it giving way unless the event says so. Louder blows past 100 are D-215; rows past Deafening are D-214.
  - **The order** (src/engine/breakThrough.ts). The action break (player and agent-run NPCs) names the title to break through to: the barrier on the way there if it has one not yet Destroyed, else the nearest wall of the breaker's title with that title behind it; or a wall by its id (a wall with nothing known behind it). A locked container is still forced open as before (BF31). The breaker works within grip reach of the doorway or of the spot on the wall, walking up to it first. The spot is the nearest point of the wall a hole fits: a hole's half width off its ends and clear of the ways already in it (beside a door, never through it); a dent already begun within a hole's width is joined instead.
  - **What they see** (BW12 a). The player's and NPCs' view of their own title lists its walls (zone.walls): id, what they are made of, the title behind only when a way from here leads there too (else unknown, so nothing is given away), and how far a dent there is broken. A way broken halfway shows as crack. The action deck offers Break through on the ways with a barrier and on the walls.
  - **Work kept on the thing** (BW13 a). Work is kept as shares of the whole on the barrier (Barrier.wear) or on a dent in the wall (Wall.dents), each breaker's seconds counted at their own speed, so helpers add their rates and anyone can carry on another's work. In a fight each turn puts the turn's time into it (no long action). Out of a fight the order runs on the world clock as a long action, stopping when someone comes into view; it breaks for at most CONFIG.BREAK\_SPELL\_S (an hour, placeholder) before it must be ordered again, and work done before a stop stays on the wall. Sneaking doubles the time as for every action (D-364).
  - **Built**: rules and materials (part 1); the order, the views and deck, holes and cracks on the plan, helpers, noise, regrowth, the tool check and walking through (part 2).
  - **Guarded** (BA6) is a tag only: the Weaver places guards with orders on that way, and the tag lets the tier counts see it.
  - **Who passes** (BA7). Code paths (routines, follow, flee) treat a way shut to the actor as no way (ActorView map links carry shut); an NPC passes where it holds the key or stands on the bolt's side. Agent-run NPCs may pick like the player (action pick).
  - **What a viewer knows.** The player's and NPCs' zone links show a barrier's kind, that it is blocked or broken, a drawn bar from the bolt's side, and a lock or a bar from the far side only once they tried it or hold its key. The Weaver sees everything.
  - **Picking a lock** (BA2). Lockpicking (SK29) has the Lockpicking function (FN15, in the Sleight group with Filching; FL1). The action pick on the way's zone rolls the function (the best effective skill with it; lockpicks in use add +25) with the lock's grade modifier, (50 − grade) ÷ 2 like every task grade (SE23; OD10 a, Ali 15/11/2026, page "Obstacle difficulty": none +15, minor +7.5, moderate 0, major −7.5, extreme −15). **Only when in doubt** (OD1 b): a lock graded CONFIG.OBSTACLE\_SURE\_GAP (25) or more below the picker's effective skill (tools included) opens with no roll, as a Success; one graded more than CONFIG.OBSTACLE\_REACH\_GAP (25) above it is beyond the picker with no roll (both placeholders, D-343); in between it rolls. Only the gap counts, so a lock at the picker's own grade is 62% at 50 and at 300. **Grade shown** (OD12 a): trying to pick a lock (Barrier.graded, ItemInstance.graded) shows its grade in that actor's view of the way or container. Success opens it once the picking time is done: out of a fight the time passes at once; in a fight each turn at it adds a fight turn of work. Near Miss: not yet, keep going. Failure: the lock is beyond the picker until its effective skill is higher than at that try. Critical Failure: the pick snaps and the lockpicks used are lost. Picking is a whisper (hushed). Time: CONFIG.PICK\_SECONDS\_AT\_50 (60 s, about a minute for a common lock) at effective skill 50, halved per CONFIG.PICK\_HALVING\_PER (25) points above and doubled per 25 below. Scaling: 75 is 30 s and 100 is 15 s. Past 100 it keeps halving (Ali, 12/11/2026): 150 about 4 s, 200 about 1 s, so supernatural fingers open locks at a touch. CONFIG.PICK\_FAST\_CAP (null) would stop the halving at a skill if set. Master or magic locks would add grades past extreme (CR4 grades).
  - **Filching** (FL1–FL7, TN1, TN2, TN4, CT4, CT5; Ali 13/11/2026). Filching (SK36, function FN16) and Lockpicking make the Sleight group (GR11, "Clever fingers on locks and purses that belong to someone else"); each stands in for the other at 0.3 (CONFIG.SUBSTITUTES). The action filch (player and NPCs, grip reach) takes one item from someone or from an owned container: anything carried and not held or worn, a seller's ware, or a share of a purse (FILCH_PURSE_SHARE 0.25), no bigger than FILCH_MAX_BULK (arm-sized). Not guarded (no owner, the owner down or yielded): it is just taken. Guarded: one Filching roll F; everyone outside the filcher's band who sees the act (FILCH_VISIBILITY, Slight since 17/11/2026: made out at 2 m, glimpsed at 6 m; TN3 open) rolls Sight against F, everyone the sound reaches rolls Hearing; the sound is Hushed, one level louder per tier F fell below Near Miss (TN1). A watcher's roll adds their attention (their latest activity: alert +13%, normal 0, distracted −13%; talking with the filcher is alert) and the item's size (FILCH_SIZE by bulk: 0, +7%, +20%, +40%), each a % of the watcher's average roll (D-76). Won and made out clearly: caught; won but only glimpsed or heard partly, or a tie: suspicious (TN2). The worst result decides (FL4): caught, nothing taken, the owner FILCH_CAUGHT_OWNER_STEPS (2) colder and every other catcher FILCH_CAUGHT_WITNESS_STEPS (1), all remember; suspicious, a fumble, nothing taken, the owner suspicious; else taken unseen. A taken item keeps stolenFrom (FL6): its owner sees it marked on the thief and won't buy it back; handing it back clears it. Opening or picking an owned container under watch runs the same contest at size 0 (CT4); a worn pack its wearer guards is only filched from, never opened (CT5). This is the first reader of an activity's attention (D-200 still parks it for sight in general). src/engine/filch.ts. Filching stays its own contest, apart from the stealth contests and the ambush (Ali, 20/11/2026, in the thread that made the ambush a Combat Stealth roll, BG-343): someone can be aware of you and still not notice you stealing from them. Any link between filching and stealth comes from perks.
  - **The tier's lean** (BA8, SD3). The tier leans the ways and never limits them. The Weaver's view carries the words (ways): Critical Failure and Failure lean toward locked, barred or guarded ways at major or extreme grades, with pursuers chasing far; Near Miss a way or two shut, around moderate; Success mostly open, minor at most; Great and Critical Success open ways and a guard who looks away. A tier past Critical reads its end row for now (D-216). Each ended scenario records the barriers its plans made (ScenarioRecord.ways), and the GM view sums them per tier.
  - **Weaver plans.** A plan's link may carry barrier {kind, grade, locked, barredFrom, guarded, keys}. Code checks the kind, that a lock or a bar fits it, that barredFrom is one of the two zones, that it is not on a drop or a frontier, and that each key holder is in the cast or on the stage.
- **Ground, free roam and chases** (Chases and Free Roam, Ali 12/11/2026: CH1–CH7, building SC16–SC19, SC23 and SD2 a; src/engine/chase.ts).
  - **Ground** (CH1, to be reviewed later: D-217). A scenario's ground is the zone the player stood in when it began, every zone its plans built (the opening plan, frontier extensions built while it runs, and frontiers its check-ins added once built), and the stage zones those plans name (links and cast slots); ScenarioFrame.ground. **Whole places (Ali, 12/11/2026, BG-43):** every zone of each place the ground touches is on the ground too (a tavern scenario's ground is the whole tavern: back yard, cellar, upstairs), for leaving and for chase ranges (onGround in src/engine/chase.ts). **Leave endings (Ali, 12/11/2026, BG-44):** a Weaver ending "the player leaves this place" counts only once the player is also off the ground. A wake-up scenario's ground leaves out where the player fell. A fallback stage's ground is its place's zones.
  - **Leaving it** (SC16). Standing off the ground with no one chasing the player ends the scenario as left, after any fight or talk (SC13); if the player is back on the ground by then, the ending lapses. Off the bigger scenario's ground too, both end.
  - **Free roam** (SD2 a). Every scenario ending starts free roam for now, so players can explore the world (Ali, 12/11/2026; CONFIG.ROAM\_AFTER lists the endings that do, every one but broken off): no scenario runs and the Weaver is not called. The world goes on (routines, travel and its encounters). Quiet-turn beats, the stall and check-ins don't run. Frontier extensions are fetched ahead as in a scenario (WB5). A road encounter starts its scenario as always, and a knock-out (broken off) still wakes the player in a new scenario; an inner scenario's end still resumes the bigger one.
  - **What starts a scenario in free roam** (WB5 and WB6, Ali 14/11/2026, on the "World builder and Weaver" page; replaces CH2 and the FR1 b gap). Checked at the end of each turn after the one free roam began (checkRoam, src/engine/chase.ts). **Danger starts one at once:** the player attacks someone or is attacked, or someone hostile to the player spots them (aware or more, seen or heard that turn); the turn plays out, then a new scenario starts with its own event roll and Weaver plan (breakCause roam). **The turn does not wait for the Weaver (BG-238, Ali BF63):** the event roll is made at once and saved (stage.starting, scenario\_held in the log), the turn is shown, and the Weaver plans in the background after the save, on a copy of the state as the start leaves it (ScenarioDirector.holdStart, startPlanning; the call is logged under the next turn, whose record holds the event roll). Free roam goes on for one more turn, people there acting as usual; nothing else opens a window or starts a scenario meanwhile. The scenario starts after that turn, with its opening, from that plan checked again against the stage (awaited if not back yet; planned then, with the saved roll, after a reload or when the player has gone to another place). A knock-out or road encounter that turn starts its own scenario instead and the held one is dropped. A window the Weaver takes starts one the same way. A frontier crossing still waits for its ground. A road encounter starts one as always. Talking and trading start nothing by themselves. **Anything else that matters opens a scenario window** (roamWindow): the player's own acts at any time (taking a job, quest\_made, or making a deal they are party to, agreement\_made: why `job` (SW6 a); reaching a job's goal: `quest`), and past the rest (WB7) an active job taken THREAD\_STALE\_MIN game minutes ago (240, placeholder: `stale`) or a quiet stretch of ROAM\_QUIET\_MIN (120, placeholder: `quiet`). After the save the Weaver is asked in the background (WEAVER_WINDOW_INSTRUCTIONS in src/agents/prompts.ts; answer {start, note}); its answer is applied the next turn (applyWindow): start, and the scenario starts after that turn as above; not now, and no quiet or stale window opens for WINDOW\_DECLINED\_MIN (60, placeholder). A failed call starts the scenario (bug code window\_failed). One window is open at a time (stage.window due, then asked; a reload asks again); danger closes it. **The rest (WB7):** a scenario that ends into free roam sets roam.rest from its challenge grade (ROAM\_REST\_MIN: none 0, minor 30, moderate 60, major 120, extreme 240 game minutes, placeholders); during it only danger and the player's own acts open a window. Storyteller styles are for later. **Why it started (SW3 a, Ali, 14/11/2026):** free roam keeps what happened in it (roam.happened: the scenario's happened lines plus the player's attacks, newest SCENARIO\_HAPPENED\_MAX). A start or a window copies the reason, those lines and who saw the player that turn into stage.cause, and the Weaver's view shows them as `target.cause` {why, happened, seenBy} (for the window call and the plan). The Weaver builds the scenario around it; the tier sets only the luck around it: who saw or heard, who comes, and facts the player does not know yet (who the person they attacked is tied to: a retired master, or nobody). Facts already known stay true. The cause is cleared once the plan is applied, or when a window is declined.
  - **Unbuilt frontiers in free roam** (WB5, reverses CH3 a). Crossing an unbuilt frontier in free roam only builds the ground beyond it, as a frontier extension, with no event roll; extensions are fetched ahead in free roam as in a scenario. A saved zone re-entered through a frontier just loads, and free roam goes on.
  - **Pursuers** (CH4). A pursuer is an active NPC hunting the player (or engaged with them) that has not broken, surrendered or yielded, and whose chase is not lost. While anyone chases the player, leaving the ground does not end the scenario: the scenario follows the chase. This is also the chase that holds an ending (SC13).
  - **Chase range** (CH5, SC19: code-run NPCs only, temporary). Names table CHASE_RANGES, least first: post (never leaves its own place: the place of its routine's first bound zone), ground (the scenario's ground and its own place), near (those and every zone that starts within CONFIG.CHASE\_NEAR\_M, 200 m placeholder, past the ground's edge by the walking path; a pursuer standing that far past it turns back, even partway along one long zone such as a road: ZR12, ActorView self.atChaseLine), far (anywhere, through frontiers after the player). The Weaver may give a band one (bands[].chase); otherwise the template's (slime far, guard ground, barkeep, patron and thief post), else CONFIG.CHASE\_RANGE\_DEFAULT (ground, placeholder). A code-run NPC closing in stops at the edge of its range (ActorView self.chaseZones). With no ground kept for the scenario, ground and near do not limit. Agent-run NPCs decide for themselves. A new range is a new row.
  - **Through a frontier** (CH6, SC17). When the player crosses a frontier while chased, a pursuer that saw the crossing learns where it leads (its last known zone becomes the new zone), and one still hunting that reaches the zone the player crossed from follows too; code-run pursuers only at range far. One that did not see it go and is no longer hunting stops there.
  - **The chase ends** (CH7, SC18, SC23). When no pursuer has seen or heard the player for CONFIG.CHASE\_LOST\_TURNS (3, placeholder) turns, the chase is lost: each pursuer drops to tracking, keeps the memory line "The player fled from me and got away." (the grudge is the price; no roll) and goes back to its routine until it sees or hears the player again (Belief.lostTurn; a lost chase does not count as a fight). Off the ground, the scenario ends as escaped and free roam starts; on it, the scenario carries on. A pursuer that catches the player starts a fight, and the scenario carries on.
  - **Scaling.** No numbers here grow with skill: who catches whom comes from speeds and senses, which already grow with skill and perks, so a supernatural runner outpaces mortal pursuers and supernatural senses keep a chase alive longer, with no special rule. A range past far (hunting the player across the world for days) can be a new row when long hunts come.
- **Looting the bodies found** (D-253 b, Ali, 20/11/2026, decision cards in the D-253 thread: "b and keep the rest parked"; who takes: "That, plus the poor"; src/engine/loot.ts). When a scenario ends, every NPC standing that found a body (down or dead; Mind.bodiesFound, BF65) loots it if its personality says it takes: hostile to the dead, valuing Wealth (VL2), dishonest (Honesty, PA1, below 0) or poor (carrying under POOR\_COIN coin, 10, placeholder). Those who take strip it nearest first, by WP6 a's rule (stripBody): the first takes all the coin, then each in turn the most valuable thing left, at most LOOT\_TAKE\_MAX each; worn containers and soulbound gear stay. The player's body is not looted here. Looting during play, telling someone instead, and the player's body stay parked (D-253).
- **Quiet turns and beats.** A quiet turn is one where nobody on the stage attacks, the player learns nothing new (no secret, witnessed fact or name), and no beat fires. The stage counts quiet turns in a row and resets after any other turn and at scenario start. Agent Weaver seeds at least one beat per scenario plan: a trigger with when quiet\_turns N, which fires at turn start once the count reaches N.
- **Stage.** A scenario is played on a stage: the set of zones currently loaded. A stage can span several neighbouring places; each zone belongs to exactly one place, and an actor's place is always the place of its zone. Moving between zones or places never starts a scenario; leaving the scenario's ground ends it (SC16, above).
- **Frontiers.** A stage grows through frontiers: labelled exits to areas not yet built. When the player crosses a frontier, code extends the stage (Stage extension) without a new event roll, using the scenario's tier; in free roam too, where it starts no scenario (WB5). A place already set up in this scenario is loaded, not planned again.
- **Loading** a save resumes the current scenario and stage. Encounter modes are chosen per turn inside a scenario. Per-scenario caps (fact promotions, minted items) reset at scenario start.
