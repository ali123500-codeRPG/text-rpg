### 4. Slots, grips and gear ops

- **Grips.** One grip per Grip part of the actor's body type (humanoid 2; wolf, dragon 0). An item or attack mode says grips: 1 or 2; a two-grip item needs two usable Grip parts.
- **Worn slots.** Armor, plus ACCESSORY\_SLOTS accessory slots (5, Ali 14/11/2026) not tied to body parts. Any number of armor pieces can be worn as long as no body part is covered twice (IR19): a helmet beside body armor that leaves the head bare. Putting a piece on takes off the worn pieces that cover any of its parts. Their weight costs (Dodge and Parry, slowing, noise steps, sleep) each count, stacked by the one stacking rule at MOD\_STACK\_EXP (IR24; `src/ruleset/worn.ts`): a mail hauberk and a kettle hat cost −10 and −10 × (√2 − 1), −14.1 Dodge. A hit uses the piece covering the part hit (stop, pool, armor skill mark). The NPC gear picker fills the body first, then a helmet over what is still bare.
- **Accessories (Ali, 14/11/2026, hub page accessories AX1-AX7).** Every body has 5 accessory slots (AX6 a). An accessory gives a flat skill bonus while worn, sized by its equipment level: ACCESSORY\_FACTOR (0.2) × EQUIP\_NEED, so Common 5, Fine 10, Superior 15, Masterwork 20, and on (AX1 d, AX2 c; ItemEffect.byLevel, ruleset/gear effectValue, which also prices it). Every accessory worn adds in full, copies included: two spectacles or two rings on the same skill both count (AX5 c; other gear still counts one copy). Rows (all Common, placeholders): spectacles (IT48, Eyesight, fragile), charmed ring (IT49), ear cup (IT50, Earshot), amulet (IT51, Resolve), dark hooded cloak (IT52, Sneaking) (AX7). A charmed ring's skill (ROLLED\_SKILL `$rolled`) is rolled once for each ring, from the ring's id and the story, so it keeps its skill for good (AX3); its name shows it, "charmed ring (Dodge)" (pieceName, in views and the Inventory). The pool is every tier 2 skill, weighted by tree (AX4 c, ItemEffect.weights): fighting and senses (melee, ranged, unarmed, defense, perception) 3, fortitude, armor, stealth, speech, trickery and tools 2, the rest 1 (placeholders).
- **Crippled Grip arm.** It keeps holding its item but can't use it: no attack, block or tool use with that arm, and no two-handed actions. A Destroyed arm drops what it held to the ground of the actor's zone; an item\_dropped event is perceived like any other.
- **Gear ops (free).** draw, stow, swap, wear or remove an accessory, pick up (from the ground or from a downed actor in the zone). Code applies them at the actor's initiative, before its action. They cost no action.
- **Drawn and sheathed (DS1–DS7; Ali, 21/11/2026, Questions hub page "Drawing and sheathing as a real action"; src/engine/steel.ts).**
  Steel drawn means a weapon in hand; a shield alone is not. Drawing and sheathing stay free and instant (DS7 a, ACT-36).
  - *Start sheathed (DS2 a).* Every NPC spawns with its weapons and shields carried, not held, and the player's starter kit is sheathed too.
  - *Code draws when it matters (DS2 a, DS7 a).* At the turn's start, before anyone decides, an NPC that is not calm (in a fight or alarmed) or is hostile to someone it knows is there takes up its steel. Anyone who attacks with no weapon in hand draws first: the weapon its order names if it carries it, else the set it carries that rates highest (`readied`, the rating BG-1583 uses); a blow of the body (a punch, a named unarmed skill) keeps the hands empty. Anyone attacked draws before it defends. A calm NPC that does not attack sheathes again what it did not draw by its own choice (`drawnOnPurpose`).
  - *Seen (DS1).* Going between drawn and sheathed is an event others see and hear close by (draw_weapon, sheathe_weapon, at the order's target when it has one). There is none when the one drawing attacks or is attacked that turn, and none for a swap or any other change of gear (DS1: "changing equipment no... I also don't need it to be mention if someone is attacking").
  - *Shown (DS3 a).* A weapon carried on someone, not concealed, shows to others and to the Narrator as sheathed; the Narrator gets each seen actor's weapons, drawn or sheathed.
  - *A deed (DS4 b).* Drawn by choice outside a fight at someone who sees it: they turn one step colder and remember it; every guard (class Guard) not of the drawer's band who sees it while the drawer stands in a settlement (the top place stands on a land site, or its kind names a settlement) is alarmed. The agents give the words.
  - *A stand-down offer (DS5 a).* Sheathed by choice before someone hostile or opposed who sees it, AI-run or the player: an offer to stand down with no words (StandDownOffer.sheathed), answered as any offer (TF15, TF11); code-run foes press on.
  - *On speech rolls (DS6 a).* Steel in hand moves the speaker's own Intimidation, Persuasion and Barter rolls by CONFIG.STEEL_SPEECH, a share of the average roll: up on a threat, down on persuading and haggling (DS8 b).
  - *On screen (DS6 a).* While a weapon is in the player's hand the play header shows a "Weapon drawn" badge beside Stealthy, in the Melee tree's colour; sheathed shows none.
- **give and take (main action; BF34, Ali 14/11/2026; src/engine/handover.ts).** Trades at no price, open with anyone, NPCs with each other too. A **give** holds the item out (from a grip, the pack or the giver's own unlocked bag; Actor.offer): it changes hands only when the other side takes it, and an offer not taken by the end of the next turn is withdrawn. An NPC the item is held out to takes it at the end of that turn when its stance toward the giver is at least TAKE\_FROM\_STANCE (neutral), it has not yielded to them and it has a free grip; otherwise its mind may take it next turn. The player takes by saying so (Ideas offers "Take the X from Y"; PlayerView shows the offer as holdsOut). A **take** is the taker's at once when the item is held out to them, or its owner yielded or surrendered to them, is down or asleep; anyone else refuses (take\_refused), and taking it unseen is filching. Both need the two to reach each other's grips; out of reach, the taker or giver steps up first.
- **Ground items (PZ11 a).** Everything lying on the ground is one list, GameState.ground, each item at a point and floor (ItemInstance.at); the title it lies in is the title of that point (groundIn, src/engine/ground.ts). A dropped item falls where its holder stands (dropOnGround keeps the same item object, so a stash marking it hidden changes what lies there). A take out of grip reach waits for the walk over to it. Saved with the floor plan on the place pages.
